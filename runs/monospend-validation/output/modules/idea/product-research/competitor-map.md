# Product Research Competitor Map

## Category map

Working category should move from "privacy-first expense tracker" to "notepad budget" or "plain-text money tracker for normal people."

Category layers:

- Broad category: personal finance and expense tracking.
- Crowded subcategory: offline/privacy-first expense trackers.
- Adjacent subcategory: natural-language expense entry.
- Specialist subcategory: plain-text accounting.
- Proposed wedge: notepad-style budget with live math and variables.

## Direct competitors

- Stroberi: strong privacy-first offline/open-source message. Threatens MonoSpend if privacy is the main wedge.
- Expense Trail: broad privacy-first expense tracker with local guest mode and optional sync. Threatens users who want simple tracking plus cross-platform availability.
- BudgetVault: free local-first budgeting app. Threatens on price and simplicity.
- BudgetNote: local-first budgeting journal. Threatens the journal/notebook angle directly.
- Oinkoin and Monee: simple offline expense trackers. Threaten on "simple, private, no account" parity.

The direct landscape shows that "private/offline/no account" is not enough.

## Adjacent alternatives

- Dengii, MonAi, and SPENDI: text or voice natural-language expense entry, often with AI categorization. They threaten the friction-reduction claim.
- hledger, Beancount, and Ledger: plain-text accounting systems for users who want durable local financial records. They threaten the technical-user niche.
- Apple Notes, Obsidian, spreadsheets, and paper: default manual workflows.
- Bank-connected finance apps: better for users who value automation over privacy or manual control.

## Manual alternatives

Manual alternatives are especially strong because the product promise starts from a manual behavior:

- Type expenses into a note.
- Keep a monthly spreadsheet.
- Maintain a text file with income and spending.
- Use a calculator for free-cash math.

MonoSpend must prove that the parser and live calculations create a strong enough improvement over these habits.

## Opportunity gaps

- Privacy-first apps often still feel like apps with forms; MonoSpend can feel like a text editor.
- Plain-text accounting is powerful but intimidating; MonoSpend can offer a friendlier path.
- Natural-language trackers parse entries but may not emphasize variables and budget math.
- A no-cloud, no-tracking, one-time-purchase promise fits the text-first privacy audience.

## Risks

- Users may admire the concept but not form a daily tracking habit.
- Mainstream users may require cloud sync or bank sync.
- Technical users may find the system too simplified compared with hledger or Beancount.
- Competitor pricing, retention, and user counts are `needs verification`.
- The landing page needs stronger proof that live math is the core wedge.
