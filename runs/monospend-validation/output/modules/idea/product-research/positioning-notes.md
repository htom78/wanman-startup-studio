# Product Research Positioning Notes

## ICP

First ICP: people who already record spending manually in notes, spreadsheets, or plain text and want those notes to calculate back. They care about privacy, but their primary pain is entry friction and repetitive math.

Exclude:

- Users who want automatic bank sync.
- Users who need shared household finance workflows.
- Users who want investment tracking or net worth dashboards.
- Users who are already happy with hledger, Beancount, or a full spreadsheet.

## JTBD

When I type my money notes, help me turn them into a live monthly budget without forcing me into forms, accounts, bank connections, or a complex finance dashboard.

## Category

Best category: notepad budget.

Secondary category: plain-text money tracker.

Avoid primary category: privacy-first expense tracker. It is true, but too crowded.

## One-line

MonoSpend is a notepad budget for people who track money by typing: write expenses, income, and simple formulas, and see your monthly budget update instantly offline.

## Differentiation

- Notepad-first interaction, not form-first tracking.
- Inline math and variables.
- Monthly free-cash calculations without a spreadsheet.
- Offline/no-account/no-tracking architecture.
- Export without lock-in.
- Simple one-time Pro pricing.

## Not-for

MonoSpend is not for bank-sync users, finance dashboard power users, shared household budgeting, business accounting, or people who want an AI financial advisor. It is also not for users who refuse manual entry entirely.

## Assumptions

- "Notepad budget" creates a stronger immediate reaction than "private expense tracker."
- Users who already track manually are reachable and willing to switch.
- Live math is enough to create a daily habit.
- A minimal parser demo can test the wedge before a full native app.
