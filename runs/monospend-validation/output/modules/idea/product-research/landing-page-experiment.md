# Landing Page Experiment

## First viewport

Current headline is strong: "A notepad that does your budget." Keep it.

Recommended supporting copy:

> Type expenses, income, and simple formulas like notes. MonoSpend turns them into a live monthly budget offline.

The first viewport should show live transformation:

- Input: `coffee 5.50 * 2`
- Input: `savings = 30% of salary`
- Output: category totals and free cash.

## CTA

Primary CTA should measure prototype intent, not passive interest:

- "Try the parser demo"
- "Use this with my money notes"
- "Join the notepad budget beta"

Waitlist signup is useful, but the stronger signal is whether users paste or type their own real expenses into a parser demo.

## Smoke test

Run an interactive smoke test before building a full app:

1. Replace or supplement the waitlist with a tiny local parser demo.
2. Let users type five lines of real or sample money notes.
3. Ask whether the result would replace their current note/spreadsheet.
4. Offer beta access after they complete the mini test.
5. Track completion, signup, and qualitative objections.

Success signal: at least 20 percent of qualified visitors complete the parser demo and join the beta list. This threshold is a starting assumption and needs verification.

## Claims

- Fact: MonoSpend currently claims offline use, no ads, no tracking, export, free tier, and $4.99 one-time Pro.
- Fact: many competitor pages claim offline/privacy/no-account expense tracking.
- Inference: notepad/live-math is the more distinctive wedge.
- Assumption: the parser demo will outperform a standard waitlist CTA.
- `needs verification`: conversion rate, willingness to pay, retention, and app-store demand.

## Do not build

Do not build cloud sync, bank connections, receipts, AI advice, multi-account depth, widgets, charts, or a full native app before validating the notepad/live-math behavior. Do not lead with privacy-first expense tracker copy unless tests show it outperforms the notepad budget positioning.
