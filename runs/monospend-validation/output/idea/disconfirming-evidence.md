# Disconfirming Evidence

## Strongest counterargument

The strongest argument against building MonoSpend is that manual expense tracking is a crowded, low-retention category. Many users like the idea of budgeting but do not sustain the habit. The market already has many private/offline/no-account apps, and larger personal finance apps can win users who want automation, bank sync, or richer insights.

The second strong counterargument is that "notepad that does your budget" may appeal to builders and productivity nerds more than a large paying market. If the target user is too technical, plain-text accounting tools may already satisfy them. If the target user is mainstream, text syntax and no sync may feel inconvenient.

## Kill criteria

Kill or pause the idea if interviews show:

- Users do not currently track expenses manually.
- Users primarily want bank import, card sync, receipt scanning, or subscription cancellation.
- The notepad/live-math interaction is interesting but not habit-changing.
- Privacy/offline is considered table stakes rather than a buying reason.
- Users will not pay one-time or subscribe for a manual tracker.
- Waitlist signups do not convert into prototype testers.

## What would change our mind

The idea becomes much stronger if users show real existing notes/spreadsheets and say MonoSpend would replace them. It becomes stronger if users ask for variables, recurring formulas, export, or plain-text compatibility without being prompted.

The best signal would be a user entering their own month of spending into a prototype and returning the next week with more entries.

## Bias check

Founder bias risk: liking elegant local-first tools and assuming other users value the same thing. The landing page is clean and coherent, but a polished positioning page can hide weak behavioral demand.

Another bias is overvaluing privacy. Privacy is a trust enhancer, but it may not be the reason people change habits.

## Current recommendation

Recommend `PIVOT`, not `BLOCK`. The idea has a promising wedge, but the current broad framing should narrow from "private offline expense tracker" to "notepad-style money tracker for people already using notes or spreadsheets."

Do not build a full MVP yet. Run customer discovery and a small interaction prototype focused only on text parsing, variables, and live budget math.
