# Customer Discovery Plan

## Recruiting

Interview 12 people across three groups:

- 5 people who currently track spending manually in notes, spreadsheets, Obsidian, or plain text.
- 4 people who tried expense trackers and abandoned them.
- 3 privacy-conscious users who refuse bank-connected finance apps.

Recruit from personal network, indie productivity communities, Obsidian/plain-text communities, privacy-focused app communities, and people who have recently commented on offline expense tracker products.

Do not recruit only personal finance enthusiasts. MonoSpend is likely most interesting to people who are allergic to heavy finance workflows.

## Interview questions

1. How do you currently track spending, if at all?
2. Show me the last place you wrote down an expense or budget note. What did you write?
3. What made you choose notes, spreadsheets, or manual entry instead of a finance app?
4. When have you abandoned a budgeting or expense app? What made you stop?
5. What do you usually want to know after entering expenses?
6. How do you calculate remaining money, savings targets, or category budgets today?
7. Would typing `savings = 30% of salary` and seeing live math solve a real problem for you?
8. What would make you trust or distrust a 100% offline finance app from an indie developer?
9. Would no cloud sync be a benefit, a deal-breaker, or neutral?
10. What would you pay once for this if it replaced your current workflow?
11. Which sounds more compelling: private expense tracker, notepad budget, or plain-text money tracker?
12. What feature would make you say "this is just another expense tracker"?

## Leading questions

Avoid pitching the product early. Do not ask "Would you use a notepad that does your budget?" before understanding current behavior. Do not overemphasize privacy unless the user brings it up. Do not ask if they like the landing page; ask what they actually do with money notes today.

Better prompts:

- "Walk me through the last time you checked your spending."
- "Where did the habit break?"
- "What do you calculate repeatedly?"
- "What would you never trust an app with?"

## Evidence capture

For every interview, capture:

- Current tracking method.
- Last real expense entry or budget note.
- Whether they use formulas, categories, tags, or mental math.
- Pain around forms, categories, sync, subscriptions, or privacy.
- Reaction to the notepad/live-math concept.
- Reaction to no cloud sync by default.
- Willingness to join waitlist, test prototype, or pay one-time.
- Exact language for positioning.

## Success threshold

Use `ALLOW_MVP` only if at least 6 of 12 interviews show active manual tracking behavior and at least 4 people ask to test the notepad/live-math prototype with their own expenses.

Use `PIVOT` if privacy/offline is appreciated but the notepad/live-math wedge is not clearly stronger than existing apps.

Use `BLOCK` if most users either want automatic bank import or have no repeat manual tracking habit.
