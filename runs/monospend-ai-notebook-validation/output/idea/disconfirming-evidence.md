# Disconfirming Evidence

## Strongest counterargument

The strongest counterargument is that the product may be too subtle. Users understand expense trackers, notes, calculators, and spreadsheets. "AI financial notebook with user-triggered calculation notes" may be harder to explain and may appeal mainly to people who already like tools such as Soulver, hledger, or Notion.

Another strong counterargument is that automatic totals are expected in finance apps. Removing automatic totals may feel elegant to the team but confusing to users who expect a running balance.

## Kill criteria

Stop or pivot if:

- testers repeatedly ask "where is the transaction list?";
- testers do not understand why calculation notes are saved;
- users prefer automatic totals after trying intentional calculations;
- users use AI Composer once but do not return to the notebook;
- notebook lines feel technical rather than calming;
- privacy concerns block voice/photo use;
- the product cannot explain itself in one sentence.

## What would change our mind

The idea becomes much stronger if testers say things like:

- "This feels like my money notes finally became useful."
- "I want to keep these calculation answers because they explain my decision."
- "I would use this instead of a spreadsheet for quick cashflow."
- "I do not want bank sync; I want a place to think."

It becomes weaker if testers say:

- "Just make it auto-categorize expenses."
- "Why not use Soulver?"
- "Why not use Apple Notes plus a calculator?"

## Bias check

The team is likely biased toward text ownership, local-first workflows, and notebook interfaces. That is an advantage for product taste, but it can hide mainstream demand risk. The validation must test actual return behavior, not aesthetic approval.

## Current recommendation

Allow a narrow MVP because the prototype is small and already exists. But treat this as a validation of a new category hypothesis, not proof of product-market fit.
