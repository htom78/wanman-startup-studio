# Problem Hypothesis

## Problem

People who manage money manually often do not need another dashboard. They need a low-friction place to think, capture, and re-check money facts without turning every note into a formal transaction table. The original MonoSpend live-math concept was memorable, but it still required users to type clean lines. The first AI Ledger pivot reduced entry friction, but it risked becoming another AI expense tracker.

The latest hypothesis is sharper: the product should be an AI financial notebook. The left column is the only source of truth. AI Composer turns messy voice, photos, and text into editable notebook facts. The right column shows derived line output. Aggregate calculations are created only when the user asks, and the answer is saved as a calculation note.

## Target customer

The first target customer is not a mainstream budget-dashboard user. It is someone who already reasons about money in notes, spreadsheets, calculators, plain text, or lightweight personal docs.

The strongest early segments are:

- freelancers and indie builders who track cashflow, invoices, runway, subscriptions, and ad spend manually;
- privacy-conscious users who reject bank sync but still want faster capture;
- note-taking users who want Apple Notes familiarity with Soulver-style derived answers;
- manual trackers who want the record to remain readable, editable, and exportable.

## Frequency

The problem appears several times per week or daily. Users capture small purchases, income assumptions, subscriptions, travel costs, tips, splits, and cashflow questions. The trigger is not only "I bought something." It is also "I need to reason about money for a moment."

That broader reasoning loop is important. AI expense trackers compete on capture speed. MonoSpend AI Notebook competes on the continuity between capture, notes, variables, and intentional calculation remarks.

## Assumptions

- Users trust AI more when it writes editable canonical notebook lines instead of silently updating a database.
- User-triggered calculation notes feel more like a notebook than automatic totals.
- A Soulver-like two-column layout is useful, but MonoSpend must differ through AI Composer and finance-specific notebook workflows.
- Some users will prefer intentional calculation notes over dashboards because the answer stays attached to context.
- The first MVP can validate this in a local web prototype without bank sync, accounts, or full mobile polish.

## Evidence

AI capture is an active category: Duly, HeyJerni, Dengii, and others publicly claim voice input, receipt scanning, AI parsing, and categorization. Soulver validates the notepad-calculator interaction, while hledger validates durable human-readable money records. Apple Notes validates the notebook mental model and, with Apple Intelligence, the broader trend of AI inside notes.

## Needs verification

The crucial unverified question is not whether AI can parse expenses. It is whether users will keep using canonical notebook lines and calculation notes for real money decisions after the novelty wears off.
