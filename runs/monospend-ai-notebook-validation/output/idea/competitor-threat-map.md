# Competitor Threat Map

## Direct

Direct competitors split into two groups.

AI expense trackers compete on capture:

- Duly AI: voice AI, receipt scanning, splitting, automations, and AI insights.
- HeyJerni: privacy-first offline AI expense tracking with voice and receipt scan claims.
- Dengii: voice/text parsing into multiple expenses and optional budget.
- Yavo, Yomio, Receiptix, and similar apps: receipt capture, voice capture, categorization, and visual spending views.

Notepad calculators compete on interaction:

- Soulver 3: natural-language notepad calculator, variables, line references, automatic totals, currency/unit/date calculations.
- Spreadsheet and calculator workflows: flexible but not money-notebook specific.

## Adjacent

Adjacent alternatives:

- Apple Notes and Notion for freeform money notes.
- hledger, Beancount, Ledger, and plain-text accounting for durable text records.
- Spreadsheets for calculations and budgets.
- Manual receipt folders and screenshots.
- Bank-connected apps for users who value automation over control.

## Manual

Manual alternatives remain strong:

- write "rent 2500, salary 5200" in a note;
- use calculator for one-off totals;
- keep monthly cashflow in a spreadsheet;
- save receipts and reconcile later;
- ask ChatGPT to parse a list once.

MonoSpend wins only if it makes this manual behavior faster while preserving notebook ownership.

## Pricing

Pricing is `needs verification`. Competitors use subscriptions, app-store purchases, or free tiers. Cloud AI makes one-time pricing risky. A safer MVP pricing path is no payment during validation, then test one of:

- local notebook free, AI capture paid;
- bring-your-own-key for privacy/cost-sensitive users;
- subscription only if users return weekly;
- one-time local app only after on-device AI is feasible.

## Differentiation

The new wedge:

> An AI financial notebook where facts are editable and calculations are intentional notes.

This is distinct from:

- AI expense tracker: capture goes to transaction table.
- Soulver: user writes expressions manually in a general calculator notebook.
- Apple Notes: flexible notes, but no structured money parser/calculation column.
- hledger: durable text accounting, but too technical for casual users.

## Threat

The biggest threat is fuzzy positioning. If MonoSpend says "AI expense tracker," it loses. If it says "Soulver for finance," it may sound derivative. The sharper phrase is "AI money notebook" or "AI financial notebook."
