# Customer Discovery Plan

## Recruiting

Recruit 15 people from segments where notebook-style money reasoning is already plausible:

- 5 freelancers, indie hackers, or solo operators who track runway, invoices, subscriptions, or ad spend manually.
- 4 note/spreadsheet users who keep money notes outside a finance app.
- 3 privacy-conscious users who reject bank sync but still want faster capture.
- 3 people who already tried AI expense apps, receipt scanners, or budget dashboards and churned.

Recruit from personal founder networks, productivity communities, plain-text accounting forums, Obsidian/Notion users, and small business/freelance communities. Do not recruit only finance-app enthusiasts.

## Interview questions

1. What was the last money question you had to work out manually?
2. Where did you write the numbers down?
3. Do you currently use Apple Notes, Notion, spreadsheets, calculators, or plain text for money?
4. When did you last forget to log a purchase?
5. What is annoying about transaction tables or budget dashboards?
6. What is annoying about notes/spreadsheets for money?
7. If you speak a messy money note, what should the app write down?
8. Would you rather see a transaction row or an editable notebook line?
9. Should totals calculate automatically, or only when you ask?
10. Would you keep a calculation answer as a note for later?
11. What kinds of calculation notes would you ask: total cost, remaining runway, split, tip, tax, subscriptions, income?
12. What privacy promise would you need before using voice/photo AI on receipts?
13. Would export to Markdown, CSV, JSON, hledger, or plain text matter?
14. What would make this feel like just a weaker Soulver?
15. What would make this feel like just another AI expense tracker?
16. Would you test it for one week with five real money notes?

## Leading questions

Do not ask "Do you like AI financial notebooks?" Ask about the last time they worked through a money decision. Do not sell the prototype before asking where the user currently captures money facts. Show transaction-list and notebook-line variants only after collecting current behavior.

Avoid leading users toward user-triggered calculations. Ask whether automatic totals feel helpful, noisy, or untrustworthy.

## Evidence capture

Capture:

- current money-note workflow;
- frequency of ad hoc financial calculations;
- whether user prefers notes or transaction tables;
- reaction to AI Composer output;
- reaction to calculation notes;
- privacy objections;
- willingness to enter real examples;
- Day 2 and Day 7 return behavior;
- exact language users use to describe the product.

## Success threshold

Keep `ALLOW_MVP` if at least 5 of 15 qualified users agree to test real or realistic money notes and at least 3 say calculation notes solve a problem they currently handle with a calculator or spreadsheet.

Use `PIVOT` if users like capture but ignore notebook/calculation behavior.

Use `BLOCK` if users mainly want automated bank import, dashboards, or passive categorization.
