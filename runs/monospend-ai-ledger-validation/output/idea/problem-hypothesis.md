# Problem Hypothesis

## Problem

Manual expense tracking fails because capture is too much work at the exact moment users need speed. The original MonoSpend notepad concept reduced form friction, but it still asked users to type every entry. The new hypothesis is that voice, receipt photos, and messy text can remove capture friction while preserving the trusted output: a plain, readable, editable ledger.

The problem is not "people need an AI expense tracker." That category already exists. The sharper problem is "people want low-friction capture without losing control of their financial record to a black-box database or chat history."

## Target customer

The first segment is privacy-conscious manual trackers who dislike bank sync but still forget to log purchases. They may already use notes, spreadsheets, Apple Notes, Obsidian, or a simple expense app. They want faster capture but still want to inspect and edit what gets stored.

This is narrower than mainstream personal finance users. Bank-sync users, credit-score optimizers, investment trackers, and AI advisor seekers are not the first segment.

## Frequency

For the target user, the problem occurs daily: coffee, lunch, transit, groceries, subscriptions, or cash purchases. The capture moment is frequent, short, and easy to skip. Receipt photo capture is especially relevant after groceries, restaurants, travel, and reimbursable purchases.

Frequency is a strong inference from the use case and from the density of voice/photo AI expense tracker competitors. It still needs direct customer evidence.

## Current behavior

Current behavior includes:

- Forgetting purchases and reconciling later.
- Typing rough notes into a notes app.
- Using spreadsheets but delaying entry.
- Using expense apps with forms, categories, and dashboards.
- Using AI expense trackers that create structured transaction records.
- Saving receipt photos without turning them into a useful budget record.

MonoSpend should aim to be the bridge: quick capture like an AI tracker, but output like a personal notepad ledger.

## Assumptions

- Users trust AI more when it writes a visible ledger line they can edit.
- AI capture is a stronger entry wedge than live math alone.
- The notepad ledger is differentiated from generic AI expense trackers.
- Users will confirm or correct AI extraction rather than expecting full automation.
- A credible privacy promise can survive early cloud AI if it is explicit and consent-based.
- The first MVP can be a local web prototype, not a full native finance app.

## Evidence type

Facts: MonoSpend has an existing landing page with notepad budgeting, offline privacy, export, and one-time Pro pricing. Current competitors publicly claim voice input, receipt scanning, AI categorization, offline/on-device AI, or natural-language expense tracking.

Inference: AI capture validates that entry friction is a real product axis.

Assumption: human-readable ledger ownership is the differentiator that can avoid commodity "AI expense tracker" positioning.

Needs verification: conversion, retention, willingness to pay, privacy tolerance for AI processing, and whether users prefer ledger text over a conventional transaction table.
