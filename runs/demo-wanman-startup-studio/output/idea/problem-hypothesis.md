# Problem Hypothesis

## Problem

AI-native founders can now move from idea to prototype extremely quickly, but the speed creates a new operational problem: they can build before they understand whether the problem is specific, frequent, painful, and reachable. The failure mode is not lack of output. The failure mode is uncontrolled output without evidence.

For this demo run, the startup idea is a local, stage-gated multi-agent operating system that forces a founder to complete validation artifacts before MVP work starts. The system treats a prototype as a learning tool, not as proof that the startup is valid.

## Target customer

The first target customer is a solo technical founder or two-person product team already using AI coding tools and chat-based research. They are comfortable with local files, command-line workflows, Markdown artifacts, and using Codex or wanman as execution layers.

Secondary customers include agency strategists who repeatedly validate client ideas and product managers exploring new AI product bets. These groups may value the same artifacts, but they are less likely to accept a local-first workflow without packaging.

## Frequency

The problem likely appears whenever a founder evaluates a new product idea, decides whether to build an MVP, or tries to stop AI agents from expanding scope. A founder working on multiple product directions could run this workflow weekly. A team with a single idea may run it once deeply, then revisit it after customer interviews.

Frequency is currently an inference, not a measured fact. The first customer discovery round should ask how often founders abandon, pivot, or overbuild ideas after using AI tooling.

## Current behavior

Founders typically stitch together chat threads, Notion templates, spreadsheets, lightweight landing pages, and personal judgement. This creates scattered context. It also makes it easy to keep the positive evidence and lose the uncomfortable counterarguments.

The local file system can become a strength if the workflow makes every gate artifact visible, durable, and reviewable. The key bet is that serious founders will accept some structure if it prevents wasted building.

## Assumptions

- AI-assisted builders feel a real pain around premature MVP work.
- The customer values explicit stage gates more than a generic startup template.
- A local Markdown-plus-script workflow is acceptable for the first segment.
- Founder judgement remains central; the product should not pretend to make company decisions autonomously.
- The first valuable outcome is a better build/no-build decision, not a polished dashboard.

## Evidence type

This hypothesis is based on the provided Founder Playbook framing and product reasoning. It still needs customer interviews. Treat market size, willingness to pay, and workflow frequency as `needs verification` until direct customer discovery produces evidence.
